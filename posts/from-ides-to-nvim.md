# From IDEs to Neovim

I've used all kinds of code editors throughout the last few years: VSCode, fully-fledged IDEs, such as Visual Studio and Intellij, and some others.
More recently, I've switched from Zed to Neovim.

IDEs are impressive. There's a lot of tooling: debuggers, refactoring tools, wizards, vast libraries of plugins, all in one place.

But... I've noticed with time that I was using maybe 20% of all of their features, the rest weighed down the experience for me:

- Slow startups 
- Performance felt "sluggish"; there was a noticeable delay when typing or navigating around the editor.
- Unnecessary memory usage
- Menus and panels I sometimes accidentally opened and had no idea how to close, due to unintuitive and questionable UI choices.

This was pretty much my problem with VSCode and Intellij IDEs. So I switched to Zed a while ago.
Zed was a breath of fresh air. It was fast, didn't feel clunky, had vim keybinds out of the box, and just generally stays out of the way. It kind of taught me that I didn't care all too much for the 500 different tools other IDEs provided, I just wanted something fast and reliable.

Some time goes by, and I start using Neovim. It was purely for exploration at first. But I kinda just stayed.
Neovim is just reliable. It's fast, portability isn't an issue, and most important of all, it just worked. It has everything I need and nothing else. And that's a good thing to me. Nowadays my setup usually just consists of a few terminal windows or tabs. Agentic harnesses, my code editor, and that's about it. That's pretty much everything I need to get going.

Despite what I was told, it didn't take too long to setup; Obviously, it took a bit longer than a conventional IDE. I also don't think IDEs are bad. Maybe they prove useful in huge codebases with millions of lines of code where you're going through 15 abstractions to find where a piece of code broke (though, nowadays, its more likely you'd just prompt an agent to diagnose and fix the issue, but it still might happen, who knows).

Maybe you're curious and want to try it for yourself. I'd start by trying a simpler, lightweight code editor for a week or two on a small project. It doesn't have to be Neovim, Emacs, or any other complicated editor. Maybe try Zed; it's quite user-friendly and felt pretty nice to use. 

Don't switch tools just to feel productive. That's just being performative. Switch if you genuinely prefer it over your old one. Use whatever allows you write good code and forget about your editor.
